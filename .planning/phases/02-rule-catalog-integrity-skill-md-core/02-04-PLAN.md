<objective>
Finish the catalog: the two remaining deletion-test rules and their reference table, the four
self-contained prose-mechanics rules, and the three protective rules that keep the catalog from
producing dead prose.

Purpose: `PF-3.2` and `PF-3.3` close the two failure classes a naive whole-phrase deletion test
silently breaks on. `references/deletion-test.md` documents the four classes as worked pairs so the
test transfers to a term the model has never seen — an undocumented exception looks like an
inconsistently applied rule. `PF-5` is the phase's answer to its own central design risk: a
subtractive catalog applied uncritically produces correct, evidenced, dead prose, and "do not
flatten" is not a testable predicate, so each protective rule ships as a presence requirement plus
a matching prohibition on check mode itself.

Output: the last nine rules, the completed reference table, and a measured final line count against
CAT-08's ceiling.
</objective>

`## PF-4 — Prose mechanics`, opened by one short paragraph stating that this section restates only
the subset of prose discipline instrumental to evidence-backed writing and depends on no other
skill, tool, or standard being installed (CAT-06). Four mechanical rules, statement plus
`**Replace with:**` only, no examples, each at or under six lines:
- `### PF-4.1 — Sentence length`: state the ceiling as a number and state the counting unit
  explicitly — words delimited by whitespace — so the limit has one definition of length rather than
  an implied one. The `**Replace with:**` half names the split: one claim per resulting sentence,
  with its evidence kept adjacent to the claim it supports.
- `### PF-4.2 — Active voice`: the actor performs the verb, so the reader knows who is committing to
  what. The `**Replace with:**` half requires naming the actor; state the one admissible exception,
  which is that the actor is genuinely unknown, and that a passive used to hide who commits is
  itself a finding.
- `### PF-4.3 — Modal discipline`: state which modal verbs carry a commitment, which carry a
  possibility, and that a sentence must not mix the two. The `**Replace with:**` half requires
  choosing one and states that a possibility-shaped verb used to soften a real commitment is a
  hedge that `PF-2.3` has already ruled out, while a commitment-shaped verb is what `PF-2.14`
  flags. Do not restate the soft modal verbs anywhere outside this rule's own body.
- `### PF-4.4 — One claim per sentence`: a sentence carrying two claims lets one ride on the other's
  evidence. The `**Replace with:**` half splits them and attaches evidence to each.

`## PF-5 — Consistency and voice`, opened by one short paragraph stating why the section exists: a
subtractive catalog applied without it produces correct, evidenced, dead prose, and the section's
job is to name the devices the catalog must not strip. Three rules, each stating **both** halves in
its own body — a presence requirement on the document, and a matching prohibition on check mode
itself (D-07). Statement plus `**Replace with:**` only, no examples:
- `### PF-5.1 — Keep an explicit before/after contrast`: presence half — the document contains at
  least one explicit before-and-after pair, which `PF-1.5` produces. Prohibition half — check mode
  never reports a contrast structure as a prose violation. The `**Replace with:**` half applies to a
  document missing the contrast: it raises a `GAP` marker through `PF-2.13`, exactly as a missing
  metric does.
- `### PF-5.2 — Address the buyer's stated priorities in the second person`: presence half — where
  the buyer has stated a priority, the document addresses it in the second person using their own
  terms. Prohibition half — check mode never reports second-person address to a stated priority as
  a violation. The `**Replace with:**` half names the substitution for third-person distance.
- `### PF-5.3 — Keep an evidenced claim unhedged`: presence half — a claim whose evidence is
  adjacent is written flat and confident. Prohibition half — check mode never reports a confident
  evidenced claim as a violation, and never proposes a hedge as the repair for anything. The
  `**Replace with:**` half sends an unevidenced claim to `PF-2.4` instead.
