# 06-08 — Gap closure for Phase 6 UAT, round 4

## Why this plan exists, and why it is different from the three before it

Round 4 found seventeen checkably-false statements — more than round 3 (11), round 2 (14) and round 1
(3). Every one of round 3's corrections was verified to hold by readers who did not make them. The
corrections work. The problem is now unambiguous and it is not the files:

| Round | Findings | Authored by the round that closed the previous one |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 14 | 2 |
| 3 | 11 | 4 |
| 4 | 17 | **10** |

Ten of round 4's seventeen were written by commit `908b90b` while it was closing round 3. The
gap-closure round is now the single largest source of new findings, and the trend is monotonic. A
fifth round that writes prose the way the last three did will produce a sixth.

So this plan has two halves. Tasks 1-16 close the findings. Task 17 changes how the closing round
writes, because closing findings faster than they are created has not worked three times running.

**The one finding that ships to a user is task 12. Do it first.**

## The finding classes, since the same three recur

Of round 4's seventeen, fourteen fall into three classes, all mechanical and all avoidable:

1. **Scope overstatement about a mechanical guard** (findings 1, 17, and round 3's own
   `publish-location-drift` and inventory-count findings). A sentence names a check and claims more
   coverage than the check's glob, path list or literal actually has.
2. **A citation or quotation that does not resolve** (findings 2, 4, 5, 9, and the `README.md`:367-369
   slip 06-07 caught itself). A line number, a range, or a quoted string attributed to a file that
   does not carry it — usually because the citing round edited the cited file afterwards.
3. **A count stated in prose next to the thing it counts** (findings 7, 10, 15, 16, and round 3's
   "four of the eight positions"). Every one is falsifiable by running one command.

Task 17 puts a mechanical check under classes 2 and 3 for the files that keep producing them.
