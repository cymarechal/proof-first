# 06-09 — Gap closure for Phase 6 UAT, round 5

## Why this plan exists, and what round 5 changed about the diagnosis

Round 5 found eighteen checkably-false statements against round 4's seventeen. The count did not
fall. 06-08's two levers — a self-audit task and a code-review gate over the round's own new code —
did work, in the direction they were aimed: **06-08 authored 8 of 18, against `908b90b`'s 10 of 17.**

But `git blame` over all eighteen refutes the frame the last two plans were built on.

| Round | Findings | Authored by the round closing the previous one | Authored by **any** gap-closure round |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 14 | 2 | — |
| 3 | 11 | 4 | — |
| 4 | 17 | 10 | — |
| 5 | 18 | 8 | **10** |

Two of round 5's findings were written by **06-05** (`README`:196) and **06-06**
(`LEGAL-REVIEW`:913), closing rounds 1 and 2, and have survived every cold read since. The fourth
standing brief is scoped to the *last* round's diff and structurally cannot see them. So the rule
this phase has been recording is not "the closing round is the defect source" but **"a closing round
is, and the earlier ones are still in the tree."**

The other eight predate Phase 6's closures entirely, in places no brief had ever named:
`evals/trigger/INIT-EVENTS.md` (02-10), `check_repo.py`'s element-label count (03-03),
`README`:53 and :306 (04-13, 04-08), `bench-deal-brief.md`'s timeline (05-02), and three
`LEGAL-REVIEW` statements from 06-02 and 06-04. Only the sweep brief, which names no file, reached
any of them.

**Zero of the eighteen are regressions.** All seventeen of round 4's corrections were re-checked by
readers who did not make them and every one stands; the user-facing derivative fix verifies rule by
rule, all 28 illustrated rules carrying a constructive line in the file the corrected notice names.

**No finding this round reaches an installed user.** Round 4 had one and it was fixed first. The
closest here is `README`:196 and :306, which a reader of the repository sees but an installed skill
user does not. Priority is therefore by blast radius of being *wrong in public*, not by ship path:
the README statements first, then the record, then the tooling comments.

## The finding classes, unchanged in kind and now measured across rounds

Of round 5's eighteen, **fifteen** fall into the same three classes 06-08 named:

1. **Scope overstatement about a mechanical guard** — **7** findings. `README`:196 ("every term"),
   `README`:53-54 (a directory the checker says is scanned), `LEGAL-REVIEW`:506-507 (a command that
   answers a different prong), `LEGAL-REVIEW`:722-724 (a machine check that does not exist), and
   three in `check_repo.py`: :997-999 ("like every other content-scanning code here"), :4470-4476
   ("This code closes that class") and :4479-4480 ("Both already carry"). **This class did not
   shrink and is now the largest.** 06-08 shipped `record-citation-unresolvable` against class 2 and
   left class 1 to prose discipline alone.
2. **A citation or quotation that does not resolve, or resolves to the wrong content** — **2**
   findings. The two derivative citations `f909d3c` broke, and the "list"/"file" correction that
   retires an accurate quotation. **The smallest class**, which is the one place 06-08's new code
   sits — and it caught neither.
3. **A count stated in prose next to the thing it counts** — **6** findings. Five reference files vs
   four, two source rows vs three, seven self-tests vs six, fifteen element labels vs thirteen,
   twenty-three keys vs twenty-four, two readers vs one.

**The three outside the classes** are a retracted phrase left standing in one of four places
(`LEGAL-REVIEW`:537), a word used in two senses in the same section (`:119-120` "answered"), and a
comparison stated in both directions (`bench-deal-brief.md`:107). None is mechanizable and all three
are what a cold read is for.

Class 3 is the one with a proven remedy: 06-08's `caveat-count-matches-constant` asserts a rendered
count against `len()` and has held. Tasks 14 and 15 extend that pattern to the two counts in this
round that sit next to machine-readable data.
