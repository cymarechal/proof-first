# 06-10 — Gap closure for Phase 6 UAT, round 6

## Why this plan exists, and what round 6 changed about the diagnosis

Round 6 found twenty-five checkably-false statements against round 5's eighteen. The count rose and
that is not a regression: 06-09's two structural changes were designed to reach text the earlier
briefs could not, both were measured this round, and both paid. The population got bigger because
the net got wider.

**What moved.** Round 5 was 10 of 18 authored by a gap-closure round, 8 of them by the last one.
Round 6 is **9 of 25, and only 4 by the last one**. 06-09's self-audit and 06-08's code-review gate
are working on the surface they cover. Sixteen of twenty-five predate every closure and **eight
predate Phase 6**, the two oldest authored by `5b124ba`, plan 01-01 — the commit that created
`check_repo.py`. They have survived six rounds of cold reads and every CI run since.

**The frame changes again, and this time toward something actionable.** Rounds 4 and 5 said the
closing round is the defect source; round 5 corrected that to *a* closing round. Round 6 says the
defect population is simply older and larger than five rounds of file-named briefs could see, and
that the single largest class in it — six of twenty-five — is the **scope absolute**: a sentence
asserting that no other code, no other check, or no other file does something, written beside the
code it describes and falsified by code added later in the same file.

**That class is mechanizable, and this is the first time this phase can say so with a command that
already ran.** Round 6 answered "does any other check read X?" by running `check_repo.py` under a
`pathlib.Path.read_text` wrapper that records the enclosing `check_*` frame. That is not a fuzzy
proxy and not a frozen literal — it is the ground truth the sentences claim. `WINDOWS.md` id 17 has
carried enforcement-scope checking as a candidate since 06-08 on the strength of one instance; there
are now six, five of them in `check_repo.py`, three inside one sixteen-line comment block that 06-09
edited and partly corrected. Task 20 decides it on evidence rather than adopting it by default.

**And one gap is in the harness, not the tree.** Three of round 6's findings came from the
orchestrator because every reader tree strips `.planning/`. The record that governs the round is the
one surface the round does not read, and one of those three — `LEGAL-REVIEW.md`:571 — was true when
written and falsified five commits later by its own round's SUMMARY commit, which no self-audit can
see because the audit runs before the SUMMARY exists.

**Task 20 — DECIDE: ship an enforcement-scope check, or refuse it and say why.**
This is the plan's one open decision and it must be settled on evidence, not preference.

- *For:* six instances this round, five in one file, three in one comment block already edited twice.
  The check is not a proxy — a `Path.read_text` wrapper recording the enclosing `check_*` frame
  produces exactly the fact the sentences assert. It ran this round and produced clean output.
- *Against:* it requires the checker to run itself under a tracer at build time, which
  `WINDOWS.md` id 17 has twice recorded as the reason for deferring. A check that re-enters
  `run_all_checks()` inside a check is a re-entrancy hazard, and the cheaper subset — task 17's
  catalogue-versus-self-test assertion — closes one instance with no re-entrancy at all.
- **Permitted outcomes, both acceptable:** (1) ship the catalogue assertion only (task 17) and
  record the general scope check as still-candidate with the six instances as its evidence; or
  (2) ship a scope assertion over a **frozen map** of `path → reading checks`, generated once by the
  tracer and asserted by a self-test, which catches a new reader of a mapped path without any
  build-time tracing. Do **not** ship a regex over comment prose — that is the fuzzy-proxy gate this
  repository has refused six times.
- Whichever is chosen, mutation-probe it against an unmutated sibling control and record both.
